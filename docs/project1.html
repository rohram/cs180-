Overview

The overall goal of the project is to align the three color channels of an image (red, green, and blue), so that they overlap correctly and form a single color image. I first used the blue channel as a fixed reference and searched for the horizontal and vertical offsets that best aligned the green and red channels with it.

Single-scale alignment

For single-scale alignment, I excluded 15% from each border when computing the alignment error to reduce the influence of noises around the edges and other inconsistencies. I then searched over a displacement offset equivalent to 5% of the smaller image dimension. For each possible offset, I rolled over the red or green channel by the offset and computed its L2 distance from the blue channel. The L2 distance serves as an alignment error which means that a smaller value indicates that the two channels are more similar and therefore better aligned. The offset that produced the minimum L2 distance was selected as the final alignment for each channel.

Multi-scale pyramid alignment

A more efficient approach for aligning larger images is to use an image pyramid. Instead of searching over a very large displacement range in the original image, I repeatedly downsample the image and estimate the alignment at smaller scales first. Since each level reduces both the height and width by a factor of 2, the total number of pixels decreases by approximately a factor of 4 at each level, as:

Before downsampling, I apply a Gaussian blur to the image. If we simply remove every other row and column, aliasing can occur because high frequency details from the original image may not be represented correctly at the lower resolution. Gaussian blurring smooths the image and removes these high frequency components before downsampling, which helps reduce aliasing. Pyramid results on pixel values

I also ran the same image pyramid using L2 distance directly on the pixel values. The search ranges, Gaussian blur, and downsampling stayed the same. The main difference is that I did not use Sobel edge detection before computing the alignment error. This worked for most images, but the red channel was not aligned correctly in church and Emir. Differences in brightness between the channels can cause the L2 distance to favor the wrong offset.

Remaining alignment issues

There is still some color ghosting around the people in harvesters, three_generations, and woman_by_yurt. One possible reason is that the people moved between the three exposures. Since my approach only applies one horizontal and vertical shift to each channel, it cannot account for different parts of the image moving separately. The Sobel approach improves the overall alignment, but it does not completely remove these outlier issues.
